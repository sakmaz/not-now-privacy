# Privacy Policy

Not Now (NoNo) • Izak van Zyl

Last updated: 21 September 2026

## What the app does

Not Now helps you commit to blocking websites on your iPhone. This policy describes the app and its Screen Time monitor extension. The copy included in the app is readable offline, including during a blocking session.

## Data kept on your device

The app saves the website domains you enter, your current or most recently saved commitment (including its identifier, timing, status and committed domains), Screen Time authorisation status, and any saved blocking-reliability message. Older commitments may also retain Apple's opaque website-selection tokens.

It also saves your deletion-protection preference and release-request timing, plus elapsed-time checkpoints and a boot identifier used to maintain timers. The app and its monitor share this information through a local App Group container so restrictions can continue when the app is closed.

## Collection and sharing

The app has no account system, advertising, analytics SDK or developer-operated server connection. It does not collect browsing history or read the contents of websites you visit. The app does not send your block list or commitment data to the developer or advertising services, and does not sell that data. It uses Apple's on-device Screen Time services to apply restrictions and schedule monitoring callbacks.

Apple's operating system, backups and any diagnostics you choose to share are governed by Apple's settings and policies. This app does not exclude its local data from device backups. This policy does not describe data handling by your browser, VPN or websites you visit.

## Screen Time and local authentication

Blocking requires your individual Screen Time authorisation. You can revoke it in iOS Settings; doing so can stop website blocking and deletion protection. Revocation does not erase the saved commitment. The app can restore a retained commitment when access is granted again and the app reconciles its state.

Optional deletion protection restricts deletion of all apps, not only Not Now. Enabling it requires in-app consent. Turning it off in the app requires requesting release, waiting at least 48 hours of verified elapsed time, then confirming. Restarts or clock uncertainty can lengthen the wait. Website commitments are separate.

Viewing the private block list uses Apple's Face ID, Touch ID or device-passcode authentication. The app receives an authentication result, not your biometric data or passcode. This viewing lock does not add separate encryption to the saved block list or hide information shown by iOS Settings or browsers.

## Retention and your choices

Saved data persists across app launches. Ending a timer or revoking Screen Time access does not automatically erase it. When no commitment remains active or retained, you can remove websites from the editable block list; a previously saved commitment may still contain its domains until replaced. There is no in-app erase-all control.

Deleting the app, when iOS permits it, removes its installed app data under iOS storage rules. Offloading is not the same as deleting; backups may retain earlier data and are managed through Apple's settings. The developer has no server copy of your in-app block list or commitment data to retrieve or erase.

## This policy website

The public policy page is hosted by GitHub Pages. GitHub logs and stores visitors’ IP addresses for security purposes, whether or not they are signed in to GitHub. GitHub’s handling of website visitor data is described in its privacy statement: https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement

This policy page contains no analytics or tracking scripts. Reading the bundled copy inside Not Now does not contact GitHub, open a website or change blocking rules. Visiting the public page in a browser makes a request to GitHub; this does not send your app’s block list or commitment data.
